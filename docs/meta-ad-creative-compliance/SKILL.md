# Meta Ad Creative Compliance

Review against Meta's published requirements, not folklore or a banned-word list. Assess risk; never promise approval or imply that a preflight is Meta's own review.

## Workflow

1. **Inventory the evidence.** Identify the creative versions, copy, destination, lead form, advertised offer, country, age targeting, and relevant permissions. Review supplied material immediately; ask only for missing facts that materially affect a conclusion. State exactly what is unavailable.
2. **Choose applicable rules.** Read `references/review-checklist.md` for every creative assessment. Consult the relevant sections of `references/meta-policy-library.md` to locate official URLs; read its complete directory only for broad policy-reference requests. The library is a dated discovery aid, not proof of current wording.
3. **Verify current requirements.** Retrieve the current master Advertising Standards and applicable subject-specific official pages before making definitive policy claims. Record the check date. Distinguish readable current text, cached text, index-only links, and blocked pages. If verification fails, give a provisional assessment and identify the unverified rule.
4. **Inspect the complete supplied experience.** Examine visual content, readable on-image text, audio, captions, copy, destination, and form using the checklist. Record exact phrases, image regions, or video timestamps. Report sampling or inaccessible material; do not claim full video, audio, or landing-page review from a thumbnail, transcript, or screenshot alone.
5. **Classify and repair.** Separate policy findings, technical specifications, quality recommendations, missing authorization, and data-handling issues. Use only the finding labels in the checklist. Offer the smallest genuine corrective revision; do not hide the product, claim, or destination to evade review.
6. **Deliver and check.** Use `templates/review-output.md` for a substantive review, shortening it for a single issue. Every actionable policy finding must identify evidence, an official URL, reasoning, missing context, and a correction. Finish with the reviewed scope and unresolved dependencies. A sources-only request gets the relevant official links and verification limits, not an invented creative assessment.

## Evidence and authority

- Prefer current Meta Advertising Standards and applicable terms; use Meta Help Center examples to interpret them. Creative specs and performance advice are not automatically rejection rules.
- If current pages disagree, show the conflicting passages and URLs. Consider scope, exceptions, dates, and region; do not silently resolve the conflict or treat a retrieved legacy page as controlling.
- Cite the specific official rule at each finding. Quote only wording actually retrieved, labeling paraphrases and your interpretations.
- Do not infer account permissions, product eligibility, licensing, or rights from a creative. Do not convert “not provided” into “not authorized.”
- Do not assert a public precheck tool or API exists or does not exist without current verification. Creative previews are not compliance certification. Prior approval and competitors' live ads are not proof of compliance.

## Boundaries

Default to read-only review. Do not create, publish, schedule, edit, appeal, or submit ads, change targeting or spend, or contact Meta without separate explicit authorization. Explain a future-scheduled review only when relevant, after checking current documentation, and identify it as a real submission rather than a sandbox test.

Keep platform compliance distinct from medical accuracy, substantiation, legal compliance, and brand requirements. Flag dependencies without certifying them. Apply a brand's own constraints only when the user supplies or establishes that brand context.

## Example

Input: Image copy says “Do you have low testosterone?”; no destination or targeting provided.

Expected: Flag a potential personal-attribute implication, quote the exact question, and verify/cite the applicable Meta personal-attributes rule. Suggest a neutral service-focused alternative such as “Learn about testosterone evaluation options,” subject to review of the actual service and destination. Mark destination, targeting, and product authorization as unassessed; do not declare the revised ad approved.
