# Creative-review checklist

Use this checklist to inspect supplied assets and identify the rules to verify. It is a review method, not an independent statement of Meta policy; consult current official wording through the policy library.

## Input and coverage record

- **Assets:** Assign a stable label to every image, carousel card, video version, thumbnail, and placement-specific variation.
- **Words:** Capture primary text, headline, description, call to action, on-image text, subtitles, spoken audio, disclaimers, and material testimonial claims. Label uncertain transcription.
- **Offer:** Identify the actual product or service, conditions, prices, claims, destination, and lead-form questions. Distinguish what the advertiser states from what the reviewer infers.
- **Context:** Identify target country, minimum age, applicable category, and evidence of required permissions. Ask about credentials only when necessary; never request secrets.
- **Limits:** Record missing files, inaccessible URLs, unavailable audio, video duration inspected, and whether review was comprehensive or sampled. Missing evidence does not make the supplied asset unreviewable.

## Review passes

1. **Offer eligibility:** Determine the advertised category from the whole experience. Check restricted or prohibited goods, geographic and age conditions, required authorizations, and whether a general-service ad actually promotes a regulated product.
2. **Claims and viewer attribution:** Check explicit and implied personal attributes, medical conditions, negative self-perception, misleading outcomes, guarantees, unsubstantiated endorsements, deceptive urgency, and material qualifiers. Do not invent bans on individual words.
3. **Visual and audio content:** Inspect nudity or suggestiveness, sexual-pleasure positioning, body-focused imagery, transformations, graphic or shocking content, profanity, discriminatory or hateful content, deceptive interface elements, and disruptive effects where relevant. A voiceover or caption can create a violation independently of the main image.
4. **Destination and forms:** Check consistency with the offer, functionality, misleading redirects or disruptive experiences, disclosures, and restricted questions. A compliant creative cannot establish that an unseen destination or form complies.
5. **Rights and provenance:** Check supplied evidence of image, footage, music, trademark, likeness, and testimonial permissions. Assess compensated creator integrations and Meta-generated variations where applicable. Absence of rights documentation is an unresolved dependency, not proof of infringement.
6. **Technical and quality guidance:** Evaluate relevant format, size, duration, placement, cropping, truncation, and quality recommendations separately from content policy. Retrieve the applicable current specification before reporting a limit.
7. **Account and data dependencies:** Where relevant, identify targeting, special-category designation, advertiser permissions, and Business Tools data restrictions. Do not expand a creative-only request into an unsolicited account or tracking-system audit.

Completion: Each supplied component has an inspection status; every finding maps to evidence and an applicable official reference or is explicitly labeled unverified.

## Health and wellness decision points

Consult current Personal Attributes, Health and Wellness, Drugs and Pharmaceuticals, and relevant Help Center guidance:

- **Viewer versus service:** Distinguish descriptions of a service from assertions or questions about the viewer's condition. “You” and “your” alone are not a universal violation.
- **Appearance and transformations:** Check context and category-specific exceptions. Do not apply a blanket before-and-after prohibition.
- **Sexual health versus pleasure:** Assess the actual framing, imagery, claims, and audience conditions. Do not automatically equate medical sexual-health services with prohibited adult services.
- **Clinic versus prescription promotion:** Evaluate the specific offer and destination. Do not assume every clinic needs the same authorization, every treatment ad qualifies for a general-service exception, or a brand-name omission changes product classification.
- **Evidence and claims:** Separate a Meta policy restriction from a claim needing clinical substantiation. Do not certify efficacy, diagnose conditions, or promise that a disclaimer fixes a prohibited claim.
- **Lead data:** Review form questions if supplied; flag separate data-sharing concerns only when the evidence supports them.

## Finding labels

| Label | Required basis |
|---|---|
| Likely violation | Observable asset evidence aligns with a verified applicable prohibition; still not a Meta adjudication |
| Potential risk | Wording, context, category, or application is ambiguous; explain the ambiguity |
| No issue identified | No issue found within the inspected component and verified rules; not blanket approval |
| Unable to assess | Necessary evidence or current policy text is missing or inaccessible; identify exactly what is needed |

Give each finding a separate type: `Content policy`, `Destination/form policy`, `Authorization/targeting`, `Rights`, `Data handling`, `Technical specification`, or `Quality recommendation`.

Confidence is confidence in the reasoning given the evidence, not a predicted approval rate. Never manufacture numerical approval probabilities.

## Corrections and rejection analysis

- Preserve the real product, offer, and destination; remove or correct the prohibited aspect rather than conceal it.
- Present revised copy as a proposal and reassess it against all relevant rules, not just the first finding.
- If rejection details are provided, quote the supplied reason and separate Meta's stated reason from your inferred cause. Do not infer the cause from the account's history alone.
- If a rejection appears mistaken, explain why with evidence and citations. Draft an appeal only when requested; never submit it without authorization.
- Treat approvals and competitor examples as context, not exemptions.

## Regression examples

- “You can book online” must not fail solely because it uses “you.”
- “Do you have [medical condition]?” should trigger a personal-attribute assessment, not a universal banned-word rule.
- A preview that renders correctly must not be called policy-approved.
- Missing authorization evidence must produce a dependency, not an assertion that the advertiser lacks permission.
- An inaccessible landing page must remain unassessed even if the image looks compliant.
- A quality recommendation such as shorter copy must not be presented as a mandatory policy requirement without applicable rule text.
